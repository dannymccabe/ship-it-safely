# Privacy basics

Read this in Phase 5. **This is a set of questions, not legal advice.** Don't tell the person their site is compliant with anything. Help them answer the questions, and if they're unsure, suggest they speak to a qualified adviser. Say "This isn't legal advice" when you go through it.

## Questions to work through

### What does the site collect?

- List every form and every field it asks for.
- Are any fields sensitive (health, finances, children's details)? If so, recommend they get advice before launching.
- Do they actually need every field? Fewer fields means less to look after.

### Is there a privacy notice?

- Is there a link to one in the footer, on every page?
- Does it say what the forms collect, why, where it's kept, for how long, and how to ask for it to be deleted?
- Does it name who they are and how to contact them?

If there's no privacy notice, you can draft a plain-English starting point, clearly labelled as a draft for them to check. Don't present it as finished.

### Cookies and tracking

- Does the site load analytics (Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, Hotjar, etc.)? Check the code and the page's network requests.
- Analytics and advertising cookies usually need consent before they're set in the UK and EU. Privacy-friendly analytics that set no cookies (Vercel Web Analytics, Plausible, Fathom) often avoid the banner question altogether.
- If they want to know exactly what's set: private window, browser developer tools, Application → Cookies.

### Where do submissions end up?

- Follow one submission: the email inbox, the email service's logs, any form tool, spreadsheet or CRM.
- Who can see each of those?
- How long is it kept? Is there anything they can delete now?

### Email marketing

- If a form adds people to a mailing list, is there a separate, unticked opt-in box? Contact enquiries and marketing consent are different things.
- Can people unsubscribe from every marketing email?

## Say at the end

"This isn't legal advice. If you handle sensitive information, or you're not sure about any of these answers, it's worth speaking to a qualified adviser."
